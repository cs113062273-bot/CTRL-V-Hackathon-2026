3. Competitive intelligence: surpass existing products

Conduct a serious competitor analysis before finalizing the architecture.

Research:

Publicly visible submissions from this hackathon.

Similar projects from previous hackathons.

Existing commercial products.

Open-source projects and GitHub repositories.

Research prototypes and academic solutions.

Established workflows that already solve parts of the problem.

Common user complaints, missing features, failure cases, and limitations.

Identify the strongest capabilities of each relevant competitor.

Treat the best existing solutions as your baseline, not your target.

If competitors have useful ideas, incorporate the underlying principles and improve upon them where legally and technically permitted.

Do not unnecessarily reinvent solved problems. Reuse suitable permissively licensed open-source components, cite them, and comply with license and hackathon originality requirements.

Do not copy proprietary code, protected assets, branding, or entire competing submissions.

Build a substantially original product that integrates and improves the most effective ideas.

Create a private competitive matrix answering:

What does each competitor do well?

What does it do poorly?

What critical user problem remains unsolved?

What features can we implement better?

How will we prove that our implementation is better?

What defensible advantage can our product establish?

Avoid unsupported marketing claims such as "10x better" or "industry-leading."

Demonstrate superiority through implemented functionality, repeatable tests, and measurable results.

Whenever a competitor clearly outperforms our product in an important area, investigate why and improve our implementation accordingly.

Do not optimize for feature count. Optimize for meaningful competitive advantage.
